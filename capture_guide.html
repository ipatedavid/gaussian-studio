Field guide · room capture for Gaussian splats · phone video

Walk, don't pan.

Splat software works out depth by comparing how things shift between frames. That only happens when the camera moves through the room. Turning on the spot gives it nothing to measure.

Pixel · 1× lens · 4K 30 fps One continuous take per job ~0.35 m/s walking pace

What the software sees

Does panning break the dataset?

Turning while standing still adds frames with no depth in them, and fast turns blur. A slow turn while you keep walking is fine. A long swivel on the spot is what breaks things. The rule: only turn while your feet are moving.

Should the passes go in one video, like a spiral?

Yes. Shoot each pass as one continuous take, so its frames stay joined. A job can also take several videos, or photos and videos together. At the end of each lap, keep walking about a metre past where you started, then change height while walking. Each lap sees the same surfaces from a new height, which is what joins them together.

Is there a known route for any room?

Yes. The usual indoor recipe is: laps around the edge at different heights looking across the room, then a loop in the middle looking out at the walls, then slow orbits around the important objects. The simulator below plays it.

Between passes, keep walking: overlap the first metre of the previous lap and change height while you move. A 4×4 m room takes roughly 4–5 minutes in total. Slower than feels natural is about right.

Before you press record

Trouble spots

Corners

Round them: walk a quarter-circle about 0.7 m out while turning gradually, as the animation does. Never stop in the corner and swivel.

Blank walls

Keep an edge, a door frame or furniture in the picture. A frame of pure white wall can't be matched to anything. For big blank walls, a few sticky notes or posters help.

Doorways and other rooms

Shoot one room per take for now. If you must pass through, go slowly and keep part of both rooms in frame through the opening.

Glass, mirrors, shiny floors

Reflections move as you move, so they look like geometry that isn't there. Keep plenty of normal surfaces in frame and don't linger on them.

Pace

About one slow step per second. If a paused frame from your video looks smeared, you moved too fast for the light.

Small objects

A pastry, a shoe, a sculpture: walk around the object in rings and take photos, not video. The object stays put and you move.

Set up

Patterned surface under it. A newspaper, a printed tea towel, a cutting board with grain. A plain white plate or table gives the camera solver nothing to lock onto.

Move yourself, not the object. A turntable makes the background stand still while the object turns, which confuses the solver.

Soft, steady light. An overcast window or two lamps bounced off a wall. No flash, and don't move the lights between rings. Glazed or shiny food is the lobby problem in miniature: soft light keeps highlights small.

Be quick with food. Pastry dries and sugar settles. Don't touch it between rings.

In the app: object scan

Tick Object scan: cut away the background under Training. An AI model (BiRefNet) finds the object in every photo and masks out the board and the room, and Spirula trains with its object preset, so every splat goes into the object and there's nothing to crop afterwards. The camera solver still uses the whole photos, which is why the patterned board matters. It adds about 2–3 minutes on the pod. Also: fisheye off, vignetting correction off (the 35 mm hardly darkens at f/8), Floater suppression Mild, or Strong for a shiny glaze, and AI geometry guidance Surfaces. Standard or High quality.

In the Gaussian Studio app

Frames per second

The app takes frames evenly through the whole video at the rate you set; 3 per second is the default, 2 for a small room. A 5-minute take at 2 fps is 600 frames, and training steps rise automatically for captures over 300 images. Past about 750 frames, lower the fps instead.

Spirula Studio

Floater suppression Mild and AI geometry guidance Surfaces are a good start for rooms. Keep one Off/Off run of the same video as a baseline.

Check the log

Look for registered N of M images. Above 90% is a healthy take. 70–90% means a stretch didn't connect, usually a swivel, a blurry burst or a blank wall. Under 60%, the job stops before training.

Live coverage while you record

What you described (surfaces lighting up as you film them, per angle) is what the simulator above draws. I didn't find a ready-made Android app that does exactly this for video splat capture. The closest options:

RealityScan Mobile (Epic, free, Android)

Shows a live point cloud over the subject that marks where coverage is thin. It's built for objects rather than rooms, and it captures its own photos, not your video.

Scaniverse or KIRI Engine

They build a splat on or near the phone, so you can check a room minutes after shooting and reshoot gaps while you're still there.

Ollidar (open source)

Draws live coverage arcs while you walk, but needs a clip-on lidar.

A custom app

An Android app using ARCore tracking and depth could paint coverage exactly like the simulator while it records. It's possible, but it's a separate project.

Self-check today: before leaving, scrub through the video. Any stretch where the picture smears, or where everything slides sideways together without near things passing far things, is a weak spot. Reshoot that part of the room as a short extra lap.
